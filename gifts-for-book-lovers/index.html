Gifts for Book Lovers

Reading lights, an e-reader, clever shelves and a couple of books about books: gifts that make reading more comfortable and a little more magical.

Curated by the Sprig & Bow team · Updated October 2026 · 13 picks

See the 13 picks Save to Pinterest (opens in a new tab)

Buying a book for a serious reader is risky: they've either read it, own it, or have strong feelings about it. Gifts that improve the reading experience itself are usually a safer, more memorable bet.

This guide covers the reader's whole routine: where they read (a good pillow and light), how they read (e-reader, annotation tools) and how they show off their books (shelves and an embosser). We've included two books about books as well, which are surprisingly hard to duplicate.

How we chose these picks

We leaned on what readers recommend to each other in book clubs, library groups and online reading communities, then favored products with a long record of positive owner feedback. For the personalized and handmade-style items, we've linked to a search so you can compare designs and sellers.

Each pick notes the kind of reader it suits, because a romance speed-reader and a slow literary annotator want very different things.

Kindle Paperwhite (16 GB)

$100+Best for: heavy readers, travelers and anyone whose eyes tire from small print

The Paperwhite has a glare-free screen that reads like paper, adjustable warm light for bedtime, weeks of battery life and waterproofing for bath and beach reading. For people who read several books a month, it can pay for itself through cheaper e-books and free library loans via Libby.

Worth knowing: Some readers simply prefer paper. If they're devoted to physical books, choose something else. The cheaper version shows ads on the lock screen.

Tips for gifting to readers

  • Check their Goodreads or StoryGraph wishlist if they have one; it's the easiest way to buy a book they actually want.
  • If you do give a book, include the gift receipt and a note on why you chose it. Readers love knowing the story behind a pick.
  • For e-readers, ask whether they already use Kindle or another platform, since their existing e-book library is tied to it.
