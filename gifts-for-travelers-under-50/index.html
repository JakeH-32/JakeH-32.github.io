Gifts for Travelers Under $50

Sleep gear, packing helpers and chargers that make flights, hotel rooms and long travel days easier, all usually under $50.

Good travel gifts solve the small problems that come up on every trip: a stiff neck on a flight, a hotel room that's too bright, a dead phone at the gate, or a suitcase over the weight limit. Frequent travelers notice these the most.

Everything here usually sells for under $50, packs small, and works for business trips, family vacations and long-haul flights.

How we chose these picks

We looked at recommendations from frequent-flyer forums, travel writers and long-term owner reviews, and favored brands with a long record in travel gear (Cabeau, Eagle Creek, Anker, Loop, Manta). We read the critical reviews for each pick to find the common complaints about bulk, durability and fit.

We skipped luggage, since people have strong preferences about size and style, and focused on accessories that fit in any bag.

We list rough price bands instead of exact prices because prices on Amazon change constantly. Tap any button to see today's price. Price mix in this guide: Under $15: 1 · Under $25: 5 · $25–50: 6.

Apple AirTag

$25–50Best for: iPhone users who check bags

An AirTag tucked into a suitcase shows its location in the Find My app, which helps when a bag doesn't show up at the carousel. The battery is a standard coin cell that lasts about a year.

Worth knowing: It only works with iPhones and iPads; Android users should look for a tracker that works with Google's Find Hub. The AirTag has no loop of its own, so add a holder or luggage tag.

Cabeau Evolution S3 Travel Pillow (Jet Black)

$25–50Best for: people who can't sleep sitting up on planes

The S3 is a memory foam pillow with raised sides and back that hold the head in place, plus straps that attach to the seat to keep the head from falling forward. It packs into a small case that clips onto a bag.

Worth knowing: Even in its case, it's bulkier than an inflatable pillow. Some people find memory foam warm on long flights.

Manta Original Sleep Mask

$25–50Best for: light sleepers, red-eye flyers and anyone in a bright hotel room

The Manta uses adjustable eye cups that sit around the eyes without pressing on them, so it blocks almost all light and leaves room to blink. It's a frequent pick among travelers and shift workers.

Worth knowing: The eye cups are thick, and side sleepers may find them bulky; Manta makes a separate Pro model for side sleeping. It costs more than a basic mask.

BAGSMART Hanging Travel Toiletry Bag

Under $25Best for: anyone who lives out of a hotel bathroom for a week

This bag hangs from a hook or towel bar and opens flat, so everything is visible and off a small counter. Mesh and zip pockets keep bottles, razors and toothbrushes separated.

Worth knowing: It holds travel-size bottles best; full-size shampoo takes up a lot of room. The plastic hook won't fit very thick towel bars.

Eagle Creek Pack-It Essentials Set (Storm Grey)

$25–50Best for: overpackers and anyone who travels carry-on only

Packing cubes keep a suitcase organized and make it easy to find a shirt without unpacking everything. This set includes a medium cube, a small compression cube and a shoe sack, from a brand that has made travel gear since the 1970s.

Worth knowing: Cubes mostly help with organization; only the compression cube saves much space. The set is sized for a carry-on, so a large checked bag may need more.

EPICKA Universal Travel Adapter (TA-105C)

$25–50Best for: international travelers

One adapter handles US, UK, European and Australian outlets, which covers most destinations, and it adds three USB-C and two USB-A ports for charging several devices at once. Everything slides into one block, so there are no loose plug pieces to lose.

Worth knowing: It's an adapter and doesn't convert voltage, so hair dryers and other high-wattage US appliances can be damaged abroad. It's bulky in a tight outlet.

Anker Nano Power Bank (5,000mAh, Built-In USB-C Connector)

Under $25Best for: travelers with USB-C phones who hate carrying cables

This small battery plugs straight into a phone's USB-C port with a fold-out connector, so there's no cable to forget. It holds roughly one full phone charge, enough to get through a long travel day.

Worth knowing: It won't work with older Lightning iPhones, and thick phone cases can block the connector. Check that their phone uses USB-C.

Nomader Collapsible Water Bottle (22 oz)

The silicone bottle rolls up when empty to get through security, then holds 22 ounces once it's filled. It has a leakproof twist cap and a carry strap.

Loop Quiet 2 Ear Plugs

Under $25Best for: light sleepers in hotels, hostels and on planes

Loop Quiet earplugs are soft, reusable silicone and come with several ear-tip sizes for a good seal. They cut engine noise and snoring, and many people find them more comfortable for sleep than foam plugs.

Worth knowing: They reduce noise without blocking it completely. Some people with small ear canals still find them uncomfortable.

Etekcity Digital Luggage Scale

Under $15Best for: anyone who's paid an overweight-bag fee

A hanging scale weighs a packed suitcase before leaving home, which saves repacking at the check-in counter. Etekcity's scale is small, inexpensive and has a long record of positive owner reviews.

Worth knowing: It runs on a coin battery that eventually needs replacing. Readings can be off by a little, so leave some margin under the airline's limit.

Graduated compression socks help with circulation and swelling on long flights, and Physix Gear's have a large base of positive owner reviews. They come in several sizes and colors.

Tips for shopping for travelers

  • Ask where they're going next. The destination decides a lot, such as whether they need an outlet adapter.
  • Smaller is better. Frequent travelers count every inch of a carry-on.
  • Pair a practical pick with a guidebook for their next destination.
