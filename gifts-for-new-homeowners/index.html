Gifts for New Homeowners

The tools, safety gear and smart little upgrades first-time owners realize they need in the first month, chosen so your gift gets used right away.

Curated by the Sprig & Bow team · Updated October 2026 · 12 picks

Anyone who has bought a home knows the pattern: the first month is a blur of hanging curtains, finding studs, discovering a mystery drip and making endless hardware-store runs. The most appreciated housewarming gifts are the ones that skip a trip to the store.

This guide covers the essentials most first-time owners don't own yet, arranged loosely from tools to safety to everyday comfort. Several make great group gifts if you're splitting with friends or family.

How we chose these picks

We built the list from the things new owners most often say they wished they'd had on day one, and favored established brands (DEWALT, CRAFTSMAN, First Alert, BISSELL, Brother) with long, consistent records of strong owner feedback.

We skipped decor, which is personal, and favored practical gifts that work in any house, apartment or condo.

DEWALT 20V MAX Cordless Drill/Driver Kit (DCD771C2)

$100+Best for: first-time owners without a real drill

A cordless drill is the tool new owners use first and most: hanging shelves, assembling furniture, installing curtain rods. This long-running DEWALT kit comes with two batteries and a charger, and it opens the door to the brand's huge battery-compatible tool lineup.

Worth knowing: Drill bits and driver bits are sold separately, so add a basic bit set. Serious DIYers may prefer a brushless model.

Franklin Sensors ProSensor M150 Stud Finder

$25–50Best for: anyone about to hang a TV, shelves or heavy art

Instead of beeping vaguely, this stud finder uses a row of sensors to light up the full width of a stud at once, which makes it much easier to use than older single-sensor models. It's a favorite of DIYers for exactly that reason.

Worth knowing: Like all stud finders, it can struggle with very thick plaster or lath walls. Confirm with a small nail before drilling a big hole.

Housewarming gift tips

  • Group gifts work well here: friends can split the drill and tool kit.
  • Add a personal touch: include a note with the date they got their keys, or local takeout menus for the first week.
  • Safety gifts (extinguisher, leak sensors) are thoughtful, but present them with a little humor so they don't feel like a lecture.
